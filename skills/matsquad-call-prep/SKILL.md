# Mat Squad Call Prep

## The document is a run of show, not a topic file

Built to glance at mid-call: **5-7 beats in speaking order** at the top, reference numbers below the fold for when questioned, checklist at the end. The person running the meeting should never have to search the document for what comes next.

Open the doc with **THE GOAL, singular**: the yes being asked for, plus the one unrecoverable artifact to secure regardless of the answer (e.g., the engagement export). If the call wobbles, everything else can drop and the meeting still succeeds.

## The beats

**1. Open with the one irrefutable number.** A ratio or an evaporation stat from their own data... "three years of ads bought 100,000 clicks; your list grew by 5,000 from every source combined." (Illustrative numbers.) One sentence. Then stop talking and let it land. Never open with a list of problems.

**2. Give the operator the win, with their real numbers.** The person who runs the systems day-to-day decides whether anything built gets adopted. Praise must be fact, not flattery... name their actual CTRs, their actual testing structure. The pattern: **praise the engine, name the missing bucket.** "Your clicks are cheap and getting cheaper... the machine that catches them doesn't exist yet. I'm not here to fix your marketing, I'm here to catch what it already produces." Nobody gets defensive at that sentence.

**3-4. Findings, numbers attached... and say what's RIGHT by name.** "Your authentication is clean" costs nothing and buys the credibility every hard finding spends. Facts only, no editorializing... the numbers carry the argument or the argument is wrong.

**5. Demo, don't describe.** A tappable mockup outsells any explanation. Send the live link mid-call: "tap through this like you're a customer." Build the demo before the meeting exists as a reason to say yes.

**6. The small ask.** Never "let me rebuild everything"... big yeses get thought about, small yeses happen in the room. One event, one system, their account, their name on it, a small monthly number, "if it works, we talk about the rest." If commissions or referral fees exist anywhere in the recommendation, disclose verbatim before signup.

**7. Pre-frame every way it will look worse before it looks better.** Costs that will rise when metrics get honest, the market being hard mode, the reported numbers that will DROP when data-quality toggles turn on. Anything that surprises the client in week three costs more than saying it in the room. Also state what will NOT be touched... running campaigns keep running; mid-season is not the time to move a working engine.

## Sections every prep doc carries

- **WALK-AWAY CHECKLIST** ... checkboxes, including the anomaly questions the diagnosis surfaced (the missing year, the mystery integration, the unauthenticated sender). Data questions make the client the expert in the room... they answer, you write.
- **IF THEY ASK** ... the 3-5 likely objections, answered with the honest caveat INCLUDED. "Raw deliverability is comparable... the win is everything around it" survives pushback; an oversell doesn't. Credibility is the product.
- **OPERATOR** ... their name, their scripted win, and the one question to ask them directly: "what eats the most of your time right now?" Their answer is the build order. Frame every system as work leaving their plate; if the pitch sounds like their job getting replaced, the answer is a quiet no that never gets said out loud.
- **DO NOT SAY** ... every claim retracted during diagnosis, every unverified search-result fact, every tempting oversell. This list is built from your own corrections, and it's the section that protects the whole engagement. One wrong claim the client can disprove discounts every right one.
- **SCOPE LINE** ... what's inside the pilot/free engagement versus what's named as paid phase two. Free work states its price out loud: named case study, recorded debrief, introductions. Never gift the migration.

## Laws

- Their data, their words. Every pitch number traces to an export they gave you.
- One clarifying question per topic maximum, recommended answer attached.
- The ask is sized to be answerable in the room.
- No motivational close. The last line of the meeting is the next concrete step and its date.
